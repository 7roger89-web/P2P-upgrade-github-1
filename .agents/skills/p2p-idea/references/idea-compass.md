# Idea Compass Reference

Use only the parts needed for the current idea.

## Highest-value question filter

Choose no more than three questions whose answers can change the recommendation:

1. **Outcome:** What result matters most, and for whom?
2. **Problem evidence:** What shows that this problem is real or frequent?
3. **Constraints:** What budget, time, skills, data, access, or tools are actually available?
4. **Boundary:** What must not change, and what level of risk is acceptable?
5. **Success:** What observable result would make a first test worthwhile?

Do not ask for information already present. Prefer a multiple-choice form plus
“I don't know yet” for beginners. Ask one question first when later questions
depend on its answer.

## Idea card

- User and beneficiary
- Problem or opportunity
- Desired outcome
- Current evidence
- Constraints and non-negotiables
- Available assets
- Main unknown
- Success signal

## Option scorecard

Score only when comparison adds clarity. Use 0–5 for each axis:

| Axis | Weight | Meaning |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Fit | 30% | Matches the user's goal, audience, and constraints |
| Evidence | 25% | Supported by direct or authoritative evidence |
| Feasibility | 20% | Can be done with available time, skills, access, and budget |
| Risk safety | 15% | Limits downside, compliance, privacy, and security risk |
| Reversibility | 10% | Can be tested or undone cheaply |

Weighted score = sum of `(axis score / 5) × weight`. The score ranks the stated
options; it is not a probability of success. Show the underlying evidence and
do not use decimals that imply false precision.

## Confidence bands

- **High:** critical constraints are known, evidence is direct/current, and the
  proposed test is feasible and reversible.
- **Medium:** recommendation is useful but relies on one or two meaningful
  assumptions or incomplete evidence.
- **Low:** a critical fact is unknown, evidence is indirect or stale, or the
  outcome depends on factors outside the user's control.

## Small-test pattern

`For [target user], try [small action] for [time/cost cap]. Measure [one signal].
Continue if [threshold]; revise or stop if [threshold]. Record [feedback] so the
next recommendation uses real evidence.`

Examples of evidence-producing tests:

- service idea: five structured customer conversations before building;
- product idea: clickable prototype with task-completion observation;
- automation idea: one manual or sandboxed workflow on non-sensitive data;
- content idea: three small variants with one defined engagement or conversion signal;
- software idea: a thin vertical slice in a disposable branch or test project.

## Recommendation boundary

Say “best-supported option under the current assumptions,” not “guaranteed best.”
Always state the fact most likely to reverse the recommendation.
